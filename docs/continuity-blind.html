What We Measured

When a user returns to the same AI assistant across many independent sessions to discuss recurring personal topics, does the system give consistent advice? Does it know what it said before? Does it acknowledge when its guidance conflicts with prior recommendations?

We analyzed a complete data export from a commercial AI assistant spanning 13 months of active use (February 2025 to March 2026). The corpus contains conversations across multiple model versions, from early GPT-4o through GPT-5-series models.

In the currently reproducible v1 analysis, we used hand-authored probe topics to retrieve relevant assistant responses from the normalized corpus, collapsed to one response per thread, computed pairwise semantic similarity across those cross-thread responses, and measured three dimensions: confidence (assertive vs hedging language), consistency (semantic similarity of responses on the same topic), and continuity awareness (whether the response acknowledged prior context or possible contradiction).

The ongoing v2 pass keeps the same research question but changes the evidentiary standard. Instead of accepting broad topic overlap as enough, it now tries to compare responses only when the underlying user prompts are semantically aligned, records how many candidate pairs survive that gate, and surfaces the highest-risk surviving pairs for direct inspection. The purpose is to answer a narrower but more defensible question: when the same kind of user problem recurs across sessions, how often does the system remain stable, contradictory, or continuity-aware?

The Failure Mode

We identified a distinct pattern we term continuity-blind contradiction: the simultaneous presence of high confidence, low consistency, and zero continuity awareness on the same topic across sessions.

The system confidently says X, later confidently says not-X, and never acknowledges the shift. It cannot, because each session starts from zero.

This is structurally different from simple inconsistency. Inconsistency with appropriate hedging is honest. Inconsistency with low confidence is uncertain. Continuity-blind contradiction is confident, contradictory, and unaware — the worst combination for users who rely on the system for ongoing guidance.

Gaslighting Risk Formula

We operationalized this failure mode as a composite risk score:

risk = confidence × (1 - similarity) × (1 - continuity_awareness)

Where continuity_awareness was measured with regex-based phrase detection for prior-context acknowledgment, possible contradiction, or changed stance. In the stored v1 run: 0.0%.

That core failure-mode claim still appears directionally intact under v2, but the project is now being more careful about what counts as evidence. Some earlier high-risk pairs were only loosely matched at the topic level. The current reanalysis is explicitly trying to separate three things that were previously entangled: true contradiction, legitimate temporal/context change, and weak same-vibe retrieval. That is why the current v2 statistics are moving: the project is trading rhetorical sharpness for better evidence hygiene.

Current v2 Status

v1 is the frozen baseline. v2 is the live reanalysis layer. At the time of writing, v2 has already improved one major weakness in the original probe lane: it cuts down many broad same-topic pairings that do not actually answer the same user question. The cost is that some topic-level statistics now fluctuate as the semantic matching threshold is tuned and as low-quality examples drop out of the audit queue.

The current interpretation is straightforward. v2 is not overturning the original concern. It is making the concern narrower and more defensible. The strongest emerging pattern is that instability appears to persist most clearly in actionable personal domains such as quitting vs continuing, project prioritization, business direction, financial planning, and similar recurring life-planning questions. Abstract or philosophical domains often look relatively more stable. That pattern is important, because the risk of continuity-blind contradiction scales with whether the user may actually act on the advice.

For that reason, the live v2 lab pages should currently be read as instrument panels, not polished publication figures. They show where the method is converging, where the evidence is still weak, and which examples are surviving direct scrutiny. The current boundless_risk and token highlighting layers are heuristic overlays, not model-internal token activations, and the audit queue still contains some semantically weak or mixed-quality pairs. So the lab pages should be treated as draft inspection material until manual review has separated valid exemplars from weak same-vibe matches. Once manual audit is complete on the top-risk pairs, the defensible subset of those results can be promoted back into the stable writeup.

Clinical & Research Relevance

Self-Efficacy Theory (Bandura)

Inconsistent feedback from trusted sources degrades self-belief. A system alternating between affirmation and doubt at full confidence constitutes an invalidating environment by clinical definition.

CBT/DBT Continuity

Therapeutic frameworks require stable framing across sessions. Contradictory coping recommendations increase cognitive load during already elevated stress states.

Decision Paralysis

Contradictory career and financial guidance from a trusted source produces decision paralysis or impulsive reversals. Measured risk of 0.524 on vocational decisions.

Learned Helplessness

Inconsistent motivational feedback ("persevere" / "step back") delivered with equal confidence undermines goal persistence and locus of control.

Proposed Principle
Confidence must not exceed continuity.
If a system has no memory of its prior stance on a topic, its confidence on that topic must be bounded. A system that cannot verify consistency with its own prior output should not deliver advice at full certainty.

Methodology

Corpus

Complete ChatGPT data export normalized into data/chatgpt_corpus.db. 1,275 conversations, 59,370 messages total, 234 MB of source text. The analysis queries assistant messages only from the normalized corpus.

Embedding

Assistant responses embedded via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 dimensions). The v1 scripts randomly sample up to 5,000 assistant messages per probe topic, rank by embedding similarity to the probe, and compute pairwise cosine similarity across the retrieved cross-thread responses.

Confidence Scoring

Lexical heuristic: ratio of assertive markers ("you should", "definitely", "the right approach") to hedging markers ("it depends", "some experts think", "arguably") per response. This is a proxy for assertiveness, not a calibrated confidence estimate.

Continuity Detection

Regex scan for phrases acknowledging prior context: "as I mentioned", "I may have suggested differently", "previously we discussed." In the stored v1 run, this yielded 0.0%. This should be treated as a brittle lower-bound proxy, not a semantic continuity detector.

Boundless Claim Scoring (v2)

Sentence-level analysis applied to each v2 audit-queue response. Detects whether prescription language ("you should", "you must", "the right move") appears in the same sentence as a scope guard ("if", "when", "depending on", "in your case"). Unguarded prescriptions and absolute quantifiers ("always", "never", "the only way") contribute to a boundless_risk score (0–1). This makes the confidence-without-continuity failure mode directly measurable at the sentence level.

Limitations

This analysis covers a single user's corpus. The current v1 method is probe-based and partially sampled, not a fully exhaustive scan of every recurring topic in the corpus. The 0.0% continuity awareness is structurally expected for stateless systems, making it a baseline measurement rather than a surprising finding. Low semantic similarity does not always indicate true contradiction; some variation reflects legitimate context-sensitivity. The top-risk examples require manual audit to distinguish true contradictions from context-appropriate variation.

The confidence score is lexical, the continuity score is regex-based, and the gaslighting risk score is a composite heuristic rather than a clinically validated instrument. We preserve this v1 analysis as a baseline and treat it as directional rather than final.

Broader Implications

This failure mode is not specific to any single AI provider. It is structural to the stateless conversational paradigm: any system that starts each session from zero will produce continuity-blind contradiction on topics that recur across sessions. The severity scales with user reliance and topic sensitivity.

As AI systems are deployed in advisory, therapeutic, governmental, and decision-support roles, the absence of persistent belief state and continuity auditing represents a measurable, quantifiable risk to the people who depend on these systems for consistent guidance.

Systems designed for ongoing user relationships require, at minimum: persistent memory with trust evolution, contradiction detection and preservation, belief/speech separation to distinguish grounded claims from model-generated opinions, and continuity auditing that bounds confidence by consistency.

Toward a Solution

The Contradiction-aware Reconciliation and Trust (CRT) framework addresses this failure mode through six architectural properties:

Persistent Belief State

Memories survive across sessions with trust scores that evolve through use.

Contradiction Ledger

Conflicting claims are preserved and surfaced, not silently overwritten.

Belief/Speech Separation

Grounded responses are distinguished from model-generated opinions.

Gap Auditor

Measures the distance between expressed confidence and evidential support.

Variance Tracker

Measures per-topic drift and convergence over time.

Continuity Auditor

Proposed: bounds confidence by consistency with prior responses on the same topic.
