Epistemic Compression:
Does Belief Structure
Survive Quantization?

Vector compression preserves geometry. But does it preserve the beliefs?

Nick Block · Aeteros Research · April 2026

Geometric compression (cosine similarity, top-k recall) is well-studied. But epistemic compression - whether the belief dependency graph, cascade paths, backward gradients, anchor basins, and self-pruning behavior survive quantization - has never been tested. We run six experiments at three compression levels and find: 3-bit quantization fully preserves epistemic structure (identical gradients, identical convergence, 92.9% anchor basin stability). 2-bit breaks it (71.4% anchor stability, 0.14 edge drift). There is a cliff between geometric preservation and epistemic preservation, and we found it.

Section 1

The Question

Compression research asks: do the vectors survive? Cosine similarity after quantization. Top-k recall after dimensionality reduction. Rank correlation after encoding.

We ask a different question: do the beliefs survive?

A belief system is not a set of vectors. It is a graph of dependencies, contradictions, trust scores, cascade paths, and anchor basins. Two vectors can be close in cosine distance and say opposite things. "I work at a design studio" and "I don't work at a design studio" are cosine-similar but epistemically contradictory.

If we compress the vectors and the cosine similarity is 0.98, we know the geometry is preserved. But do the dependency edges still hold? Does backward error propagation still produce the correct gradients? Do wrong beliefs still self-prune? Do the anchor basins - the gravitational centers of the belief space - maintain their structure?

Geometric compression is well-studied. Epistemic compression - whether the belief structure survives quantization - is, to our knowledge, untested in prior work.

These numbers look good. But they measure geometric preservation - did the vectors stay close? They do not measure:

Epistemic Property 1
Edge Preservation
Do SUPPORTS and CONTRADICTS edges in the BRG still represent valid relationships after the vectors shift?
Epistemic Property 2
Gradient Preservation
Does backward influence propagation produce the same error gradients on compressed vectors?
Epistemic Property 3
Self-Pruning
Do wrong beliefs still die from accumulated error signals after compression?
Epistemic Property 4
Anchor Basin Stability
Do the gravitational basins around high-trust anchors maintain their structure? Do non-anchor memories still orbit the same poles?
Epistemic Property 5
Convergence
Does the bidirectional system (forward cascade + backward backprop) still converge to a stable fixed point?

A system that compresses vectors but destroys belief structure has optimized the wrong thing.

Section 3

Method

We construct a 17-node BRG modeled on production data: identity anchors (name, health), preference contradictions (orange vs green, coffee vs never-coffee), a wrong belief with three upstream supporters (the design studio problem), and self-model nodes. 15 typed edges with known weights.

We build this graph three times, each with the same topology but different vector precision:

Section 4

Results

3-bit Quantization: Full Epistemic Preservation

Lab 8: Edge Preservation

15 edges · 3-bit quantization

3/3 Edge sets identical. Mean similarity drift 0.015, max 0.031. All SUPPORTS relationships remain semantically valid after compression.

Lab 9: Gradient Preservation

Backward pass from design_studio correction · Loss = 1.023

2/2 Same 4 nodes affected. Gradient difference: 0.000000 across all nodes. This is expected by design: gradients flow through edge weights and damping constants, which are stored independently of the vectors. The result confirms the architecture separates epistemic mechanics (topology-dependent) from retrieval mechanics (geometry-dependent).

Lab 10: Self-Pruning

Design studio belief · 4 correction cycles

2/2 Both full-precision and 3-bit compressed: design_studio trust reaches 0.050 after 3 corrections. Identical pruning trajectory. Wrong beliefs die the same way regardless of vector precision.

Lab 11: Anchor Basin Stability

3 anchors (name, leukemia, orange) · 14 non-anchor memories

2/2 92.9% of non-anchor memories keep the same nearest anchor after 3-bit compression. All 7 anchor-satellite similarity pairs within 0.05 drift. One flip: left_walmart shifted from identity basin to health basin - an employment fact sitting at the boundary between two semantic poles.

Lab 12: Convergence

5 corrections across 3 domains

2/2 Contraction ratio: 0.5950 for both full and compressed. Difference: 0.0000. The bidirectional system converges identically under compression.

Key Finding
Epistemic mechanics and retrieval mechanics have different compression sensitivities.
Trust propagation (forward cascade, backward influence) depends on edge weights and damping - topology, not geometry. These survive any compression level. Retrieval, contradiction detection, and domain classification depend on vector proximity - geometry. These break at 2-bit. The architecture naturally separates what is compressible from what is not.

Stress Test: Where It Breaks

2-bit Quantization: Epistemic Degradation

Same graph, 4-level quantization · Cosine 0.870

0/4 Mean edge drift 0.042 (2.8x worse than 3-bit). Max edge drift 0.141 - exceeds the 0.10 safety threshold. 4 anchor flips out of 14 non-anchors (71.4% stability). The gravitational basins start merging. Employment facts migrate between identity and health basins. Preferences drift toward identity. The belief space loses its structure.

PCA-96: Perfect Epistemic Preservation

SVD reduction to 96 dimensions, reconstructed to 384D

4/4 100% anchor assignment stability. 0.000 drift on every metric. Dimensionality reduction preserves the principal components that define basin structure. The epistemic topology lives in the top 96 dimensions.

There is a sharp boundary between geometric preservation and epistemic preservation.

The cliff is between 3-bit and 2-bit. At 3-bit (cosine 0.97), the belief graph is fully functional. At 2-bit (cosine 0.87), anchor basins merge, employment facts drift between semantic poles, and the belief space loses the geometric separation that makes dependency edges meaningful.

Note: Gradients, self-pruning, and convergence are preserved even at 2-bit because they depend on graph topology (edge weights, damping constants), not vector geometry. The things that break are the semantic properties: which memories belong to which domain, which anchor they orbit, whether a SUPPORTS edge still represents semantic proximity. The topology survives. The semantics of the topology degrade.

This has direct production implications. The backward influence propagation mechanism is safe under compression because it is topology-dependent. But retrieval (which anchor basin does this query land in?), contradiction detection (are these two memories semantically opposed?), and domain classification (which volatility bucket does this correction go in?) are geometry-dependent and will fail at 2-bit.

Query resonance (wobble) - where activation propagates through BRG edges to surface cross-domain facts - was separately validated under compression: 7/8 top wobble memories preserved at 3-bit, 89% of edge-propagated facts maintained. The wobble pattern is topology-dependent (like gradients), so it survives the same compression that preserves the BRG. The belief loci geometry defines the anchor basins that organize this topology.

Section 6

Implications

Implication 1
CogniMap Strategy Is Validated
The trust-weighted compression strategy - anchors at full precision, low-trust memories at PCA-96 - is epistemically safe. Anchors maintain their gravitational structure. Low-trust memories compressed via PCA-96 show perfect preservation (100% basin stability). The 6.5x compression ratio from the CogniMap experiments carries no epistemic cost.
Implication 2
3-bit Is the Epistemic Floor
For uniform quantization, 3-bit is the minimum safe level. Below this, anchor basins merge and domain boundaries blur. Above this, marginal gains. The sweet spot identified in the original compression labs (3-bit, 10.4x compression) is confirmed as epistemically sound.
Implication 3
Trust Propagation Is Compression-Immune (By Design)
The backward influence pass produces identical results at every compression level because edge weights are stored independently of vectors. This is a consequence of the architecture, not a surprising discovery - but it confirms that the design correctly separates topology-dependent operations (trust propagation) from geometry-dependent operations (retrieval). The entire trust evolution mechanism works on compressed memory with zero degradation.
Implication 4
Boundary Memories Are Vulnerable
The one anchor flip at 3-bit (left_walmart drifting from identity to health basin) reveals that memories sitting at the boundary between two semantic poles are compression-sensitive. Employment facts that relate to both identity and health are the first to flip. A production system should track these boundary memories and protect them from aggressive compression.
Implication 5
Cosine Similarity Is Insufficient
2-bit achieves 0.87 cosine similarity - a number that looks acceptable by standard compression metrics. But the belief structure is degraded: 4 anchor flips, 0.14 edge drift, basins merging. Cosine similarity does not measure epistemic preservation. A new metric is needed: anchor basin assignment stability, which directly measures whether the belief topology survives.
The Result
Epistemic structure is compressible.
But the metric for success is not cosine similarity.
The question is not "did the vectors survive?" The question is "did the beliefs survive?" At 3-bit, they do. At 2-bit, they don't. The cliff is sharp, measurable, and invisible to standard compression benchmarks.
