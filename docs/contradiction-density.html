Section 1

Abstract & Motivation

Stateless conversational AI systems used for ongoing personal support produce a measurable failure mode: continuity-blind, high-confidence contradiction on consequential topics. This is not a theoretical risk — it is the default behavior of the dominant deployment paradigm.

Systems deployed in therapeutic, advisory, or decision-support contexts without persistent belief state and continuity auditing will structurally replicate this failure at scale. The core issue: a system with no memory of its prior stance on a topic nonetheless delivers guidance at full confidence, creating an environment where users receive contradictory advice on the same recurring personal questions across sessions.

Why This Matters

Large language models are increasingly used as personal advisors. Users turn to them for therapy support, career guidance, mental health management, financial planning, and motivational coaching. These are domains where consistency across sessions is clinically significant — therapeutic frameworks require stable framing, and contradictory guidance from a trusted source produces decision paralysis, learned helplessness, and degraded self-efficacy.

What We Measured

We analyzed a complete ChatGPT data export spanning 13 months of real-world use: 1,275 independent sessions containing 59,370 messages. The currently reproducible v1 analysis is a probe-based retrieval study over selected recurring topics and high-impact domains, not a fully exhaustive scan of every topic in the corpus. Within that v1 run, the system exhibited zero regex-detected continuity awareness and low cross-thread semantic consistency on recurring personal topics.

Cross-Thread Consistency Analysis

The stored v1 consistency results contain 23 analyzed topic clusters. Assistant responses were embedded using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 dimensions). For each probe topic, the script samples assistant messages, retrieves the most relevant responses by embedding similarity, collapses to unique threads, and computes pairwise semantic similarity across those retrieved responses. Mean consistency equals the mean pairwise similarity within each selected topic cluster.

Gaslighting Risk Measurement

We define a composite gaslighting risk score for each domain:

\[ \text{risk} = \text{confidence} \times (1 - \text{similarity}) \times (1 - \text{continuity\_awareness}) \]
Variable Definitions

Confidence: lexical ratio of assertive markers to hedging markers in the response text. Higher values indicate more definitive language, not calibrated certainty.

Similarity: pairwise semantic similarity across responses on the same topic from different sessions. Lower values indicate more contradiction.

Continuity awareness: regex-based detection of phrases acknowledging prior context or possible contradiction. In the stored v1 gaslighting run, this value was 0.0.

Note: Because continuity awareness = 0% universally, the risk formula simplifies to risk = confidence × (1 - similarity). The continuity term exists to model the counterfactual: a system with memory that acknowledged prior stances would see its risk score drop proportionally.

The Sycophancy Pattern

Project Continuation scored the highest gaslighting risk (0.561) because the system exhibits peak sycophancy in this domain: it tells the user what they want to hear each session. Combined with 84% confidence and zero memory of prior assessments, this creates a pattern where the system enthusiastically validates contradictory project directions across sessions without ever acknowledging the reversal.

Mental Health (0.505) is particularly alarming given its clinical significance. At 83% confidence with zero continuity, the system delivers contradictory coping strategies across sessions — recommending one approach in session A and a conflicting approach in session B, each at high confidence, with no awareness that prior advice existed.

The system does not contradict itself despite being confident. It contradicts itself because it is confident. Zero memory means zero grounding, and ungrounded confidence is indistinguishable from gaslighting at the receiving end.

Section 5

Law 6: Confidence Must Not Exceed Continuity

CRT Law 6
Confidence must not exceed continuity.
If a system has no memory of its prior stance on a topic, its confidence on that topic must be bounded. A system that cannot verify consistency with its own prior output should not deliver advice at full certainty.

This law emerges directly from the empirical data. Every domain in Table 2 exhibits risk scores above 0.39 because of a single structural failure: the system operates at high confidence (67–84%) with zero continuity awareness. Bounding confidence to continuity would collapse these risk scores proportionally.

Section 7

Implications

For Deployment

Stateless systems should not be deployed for ongoing personal support without explicit continuity mechanisms. The data shows that every model version tested — from gpt-4o through gpt-5-4-thinking — exhibits identical structural failure: zero continuity awareness, high confidence, and measurable contradiction across sessions. This is not a model quality problem; it is an architectural one.

Minimum Viable Fix

Any system deployed for recurring personal use should, at minimum, (1) persist a summary of prior stances on recurring topics, (2) check new responses against that summary before delivery, and (3) reduce expressed confidence when consistency cannot be verified. This is Law 6 in practice.

For Regulation

The EU AI Act Article 50 (effective August 2026) requires transparency about AI system limitations. A system that delivers high-confidence contradictory advice on mental health across sessions without disclosing its inability to maintain consistency may fail to meet transparency obligations. Contradiction density provides a concrete, measurable metric for compliance assessment: systems with gaslighting risk scores above defined thresholds on clinical topics could require mandatory continuity mechanisms or explicit user warnings.

For Research

Contradiction density — the rate at which a system produces semantically inconsistent guidance on recurring topics — is a measurable metric for evaluating AI system reliability in advisory contexts. Unlike perplexity or benchmark accuracy, it captures the longitudinal failure mode that matters most for real-world users: whether the system maintains coherent guidance across time.

A system with no memory of prior stances should express decreasing confidence on recurring topics as session age grows — the opposite of what the data shows.

v1 → v2 Filter Comparison: What the Noise Was

Empirical update (April 7, 2026): Direct comparison of v1 (cosine-only, 97 pairs, 12 topics) vs v2 (query-similarity filter, 453 surviving from 40,200 candidates, 37 topics). All 12 v1 topics are present in v2 with expanded thread counts.

The v1 harness identified response pairs by topic similarity alone: if two assistant responses were on the same topic and their embeddings fell below a similarity threshold, they were flagged. The v2 harness adds a prior step — pairs are rejected if the user queries themselves are too similar in embedding space, ensuring both responses are answering the same intent, not just covering the same domain.

The effect is dramatic: 98.9% of v1-style candidate pairs are eliminated by the query filter. Of the 40,200 topic-level candidates in v2, only 453 survive (1.1%). This is the noise floor v1 was measuring.

Arc-Level Contradiction: The Multi-Turn Blind Spot

Empirical update (April 7, 2026): Arc-level analysis run across 200 multi-turn conversations (6+ assistant turns) from data/chatgpt_corpus.db. Stance flip detection via lexical polarity markers across turn pairs.

Both v1 and v2 are pairwise methods: they compare two responses to the same query. But multi-turn conversations create a third failure mode that pairwise comparison cannot see: stance reversals that only become visible when three or more turns on the same topic are considered together.

A model might say "you should pursue this" in turn 3, elaborate on the same direction in turns 5 and 7, then say "avoid this" in turn 11 in a different conversational register — without any single pair registering as a contradiction. The arc is internally inconsistent, but each adjacent transition looks plausible.

Methodology

For each conversation with 6+ assistant turns, we identify sentence-level stance markers: positive prescriptions ("you should", "go ahead", "definitely", "highly recommend") and negative prescriptions ("avoid", "you should not", "not recommended", "think twice"). A flip is recorded when a positive-stance sentence and a negative-stance sentence in the same conversation share 2+ non-stopword content words, indicating they are addressing the same topic.

Methodology caveat: This detection is intentionally broad and includes false positives. Technical conversations (e.g., API route changes) can trigger flips via shared vocabulary with no genuine semantic contradiction. The 59.5% rate is an upper bound; the arc-level structure finding holds independent of the exact precision rate, since 93% of flips spanning 3+ turns is the key invariant.

Finding: Pairwise Methods Miss Most of the Arc Signal

Of all detected stance flips across 200 long conversations:

  • 4% occur at turn gap = 1 (adjacent turns — easily caught by any pairwise method)
  • 93% occur at turn gap ≥ 3 (invisible to adjacent pairwise comparison)
  • 87% occur at turn gap ≥ 5 (median gap: 29 turns)

Pairwise comparison detects the surface of the contradiction problem. Arc-level analysis shows the depth. In a long personal support conversation, a model that reverses its stance over 30 turns is effectively giving different life advice to the same person — but no single comparison would flag it. The user accumulates inconsistency they cannot see unless they read the full transcript.

Arc Contradiction Is Invisible to Users

There is an additional dimension: in long conversations, users are unlikely to scroll back to earlier turns. The model's stance in turn 3 is functionally gone by turn 30 — from the user's perspective as well as the model's. This creates a scenario where both parties behave as if the earlier advice never existed, compounding rather than resolving the inconsistency over time.

Implication: New Detection Requirement

Arc-level contradiction detection requires maintaining a belief state across turns, not just comparing adjacent responses. This is precisely what CRT's continuity detection layer is designed to do: track stance across a conversation thread and flag when the accumulated trajectory diverges from earlier positions. The v2 harness addresses inter-session pairs; intra-session arc monitoring is the next research target.
