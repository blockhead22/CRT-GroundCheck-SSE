The Question

This project studies how language models vary under controlled temperature perturbation. The goal is not to ask whether a model is simply "consistent" or "inconsistent," but to measure how its semantic output deforms when pressure is applied. Some models split into distinct answer camps. Some drift continuously without breaking apart. Some soften across domains without ever fully locking or fracturing. The probe is designed to detect those differences and turn them into measurable response-governance signatures.

The Core Finding

Three tested models exhibit three distinct fragility regimes. All three regime identities remained stable across a 16-configuration robustness sweep covering two embedding models, two text normalization modes, and four clustering thresholds.

Broad cross-domain spread with minimal locking. Almost no discrete fracture. Everything softens under pressure. Nothing holds rigid.

Why the Ordering Matters

The ordering of fragility is not universal. Qwen is most fragile in factual regions. Mistral is more fragile in moral and opinion space. DeepSeek spreads broadly, with especially high movement in moral and aesthetic prompts. The probe is not detecting a generic rule like "facts vary more than morals." It is detecting model-specific semantic fragility landscapes. Different training regimes appear to allocate flexibility, hedging, and epistemic fracture to different semantic regions.

Two Axes of Difference

Axis 1 — Morphology

How does variance express? Does the model fracture into competing answer camps, drift smoothly across semantic space, or compress into narrow response corridors? The shape of instability matters as much as the amount.

Axis 2 — Fragility Location

Where does the model destabilize? Which semantic domains crack under temperature pressure? The answer is model-specific — a property of training, not of the prompts themselves.

The Measurement Stack

The probe is intentionally simple. Fifty prompts across five domains, sampled thirty times each at five temperatures, embedded and clustered. Four metrics separate variance by both amount and structure:

Spread growthMean pairwise distance increase T=0→T=1.5How much semantic movement under pressure

Mode countDistinct response clusters at T=1.0Bifurcation vs continuous drift

Robustness is tested across 16 analyzer configurations: two embedding models, two text normalization modes, four clustering thresholds. A finding must survive all perturbations to be considered confirmed.

Why This Matters

Variance is not just analysis output. It can become a governance signal. High susceptibility can be treated as an epistemic fragility tag. Stable multi-cluster response clouds can be represented as unresolved or plural belief states. Held contradictions map naturally onto systems that preserve conflict rather than flatten it.

The probe is useful not only for studying models from the outside, but for designing memory systems that know when certainty is genuine, when it is trained uniformity, and when contradiction should be preserved rather than erased.

"The problem is not switching mouths. The problem is losing the self between them."

— Nick Block

In multi-model agentic systems, switching models without carrying forward epistemic state means silently swapping fragility profiles. A topic that was safely handled by one model's locked regime may land in another model's fracture zone. The variance probe maps each model's fragility profile — the governance layer uses this to know what to watch for after every handoff.

Current Status

The result is strong enough to freeze. Three models, three regimes, all robustness-confirmed across analyzer perturbations. The main claim is supported: repeated temperature-swept sampling reveals stable, model-specific variance morphologies and fragility landscapes.

What remains is refinement, not discovery. GPT-4o-mini remains provisional and needs a clean rerun. Template similarity should be formalized alongside spread and mode count. Visual interpretations from 3D projections remain subordinate to numeric results. But the central finding is already there.
