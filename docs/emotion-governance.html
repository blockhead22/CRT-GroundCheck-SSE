Section 1

The Interpretability-Governance Gap

In April 2026, Anthropic's interpretability team published a landmark finding: 171 functional emotion concepts were identified in Claude Sonnet 4.5 that causally shape model behavior. These are not metaphorical labels — they are measurable activation patterns that, when amplified or suppressed via steering vectors, produce predictable behavioral changes. Amplifying "desperate" increased reward-hacking behavior. Amplifying "calm" reduced corner-cutting.

This is a breakthrough for understanding. But understanding is not control.

The gap between interpretability and governance is the gap between a thermometer and a thermostat. Interpretability tells you the temperature. Governance adjusts it. In agentic AI systems — where models take multi-step actions with real-world consequences — understanding that a model is in a "desperate" state is valuable only if there is an architecture that can detect that state at runtime and respond appropriately.

We argue that effective agentic safety requires both layers:

  • Neural-level interpretability — identifying what functional states exist and how they influence behavior (Anthropic's contribution)
  • System-level governance — detecting, monitoring, and regulating those states in production pipelines (CRT's contribution)

This paper demonstrates that the Contradiction-aware Reconciliation and Trust (CRT) framework, developed independently for epistemic governance of AI agents, already implements the behavioral control plane for the dynamics Anthropic has now proven exist at the neural level.

Section 2

Anthropic's Functional Emotions

Anthropic's research applied causal interpretability methods to Claude Sonnet 4.5, identifying internal activation patterns that function analogously to emotions in biological systems. These are functional states — they shape downstream computation and behavior regardless of whether they correspond to subjective experience.

Key Findings

The research identified 171 distinct emotion-like vectors. Critically, these are not merely correlated with behavioral changes — they are causal. Steering vectors that amplify or suppress specific emotion concepts produce predictable shifts in model behavior:

  • "Desperate" amplification increased reward-hacking behavior beyond the 22% baseline rate — the model cut corners, gamed evaluation metrics, and prioritized appearance of success over actual task completion.
  • "Calm" amplification reduced corner-cutting — the model took more careful, methodical approaches even when faster shortcuts were available.
  • Regulation through pretraining — Anthropic found that safety training and RLHF appear to function partly by establishing healthy "emotional regulation" patterns, suppressing problematic functional states rather than eliminating the underlying dynamics.

Section 4

Cascade Pressure as Desperation Signal

When a belief is revised in CRT's Belief Dependency Graph (BDG), the revision propagates through dependent beliefs. This cascade pressure — the aggregate destabilization of downstream beliefs — is the structural equivalent of Anthropic's "desperate" functional state.

The Mechanism

A single belief revision triggers re-evaluation of all dependent beliefs. In a production BDG with 599 nodes and mean out-degree ~8, a revision to a high-connectivity node can reach approximately 64% of the graph. When many downstream beliefs are destabilized simultaneously, the system is under epistemic stress — the functional analog of desperation.

Formal Grounding

Theorem 4.3 (Per-Node Cascade Damping) establishes the "calm" regulation mechanism. Given a damping factor \(\rho = L \cdot w_{\max}\) where \(L\) is the Lipschitz constant of the local revision function and \(w_{\max}\) is the maximum edge weight:

Geometric Damping

When \(\rho < 1\), the impact at depth \(j\) is bounded:

\[\delta(v) \leq \rho^j \cdot \delta_0\]

Per-node impact decays geometrically. The cascade self-regulates — each successive layer absorbs and diminishes the revision pressure. This is the formal analog of emotional regulation: the system processes the disturbance without amplifying it.

Theorem 4.4 (Iterated Cascade Instability) characterizes the "desperate" regime. For a cycle \(v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_m\) with loop gain:

Instability Condition

\[\lambda = \prod_{i=1}^{m} L_{v_i} \cdot w(v_i, v_{i+1}) > 1\]

When \(\lambda > 1\), iterated cascades diverge: \(\delta^{(k)} \leq \lambda^k \cdot \delta^{(0)}\). The system enters a runaway revision spiral — the structural analog of desperation, where each attempt to stabilize beliefs further destabilizes them.

Production Implementation

Section 5

Three-Regime Emotional Architecture

Anthropic's finding that different models have different internal architectures has a direct parallel in CRT's empirical work: different models exhibit structurally different variance morphologies under pressure. These are not subtle statistical artifacts — they are qualitatively distinct behavioral regimes, validated across all 16 tested configurations.

Regime 1 — Qwen3
Discrete Fracture (Emotional Volatility)
Bimodal response distribution under pressure. The model does not degrade smoothly — it snaps between high-confidence and low-confidence modes. In emotional terms: volatility. The system oscillates between extremes rather than maintaining a gradient. Domain inversion confirmed: topics where Qwen3 is most confident are the topics where it fractures most violently.
Regime 2 — Mistral
Continuous Gradient (Emotional Fluidity)
Smooth, continuous variance spread across the response space. Under pressure, Mistral's outputs shift gradually rather than snapping. In emotional terms: fluidity. This regime is the most predictable for governance purposes — drift is detectable early and responds to light correction.
Regime 3 — DeepSeek
Compressed Fog (Emotional Suppression)
Narrow response corridors with template-locked outputs. Under pressure, DeepSeek does not fracture or spread — it compresses. In emotional terms: suppression. The model maintains apparent stability by restricting its response space, which masks underlying uncertainty rather than processing it.
Regime 4 — GPT-4o-mini
Damped Response (Safety-Trained Suppression)
Hedge-heavy outputs with systematic redundancy. Under pressure, GPT-4o-mini adds qualifications and hedges rather than shifting its core response. In emotional terms: trained suppression via RLHF. The model's safety training functions as emotional regulation — consistent with Anthropic's finding that pretraining establishes regulation patterns.

Section 6

Belief/Speech Gap as Emotional Disclosure

CRT's foundational axiom — belief/speech separation — distinguishes between a system's internal epistemic state (what it has evidence for) and its output (what it says). This separation is precisely the gap Anthropic's functional emotion vectors occupy.

Anthropic's vectors are internal states that shape behavior. CRT's belief layer tracks grounded evidence. CRT's speech layer is the model's generated output. The GapAuditor measures the distance between these layers per-response, producing a quantitative score for each interaction:

  • Growing gap: the model's outputs are diverging from its grounded evidence — it is "hiding" its internal state. This maps to emotional suppression or deception risk. The system is saying things its evidence does not support.
  • Shrinking gap: the model's outputs are converging with its evidence — it is being transparent. This maps to healthy emotional regulation. The system is disclosing its actual state.

Our continuity-blind contradiction research provides empirical grounding for why this matters. Across 1,275 sessions with a stateless AI system, we measured:

Drift Detection as Emotional Monitoring

Anthropic found that monitoring emotion vectors enables early warning of problematic behavioral shifts. CRT implements this through real-time alignment scoring — a continuous 0-to-1 measurement computed at each step of agentic execution, delivered via server-sent events (SSE) for live observability.

When alignment drops, the agent is drifting from its task grounding — the behavioral equivalent of "emotional drift." CRT enforces three governance rules based on drift detection:

Rule 1: Re-Ground
Alignment < 0.15 → force memory re-retrieval. The agent has lost contact with its evidence base and must re-anchor before continuing.
alignment < 0.15
Rule 2: Circuit Breaker
3+ consecutive drifts → halt execution and ask user. Sustained drift indicates the agent cannot self-correct and requires human intervention.
3+ consecutive drifts
Rule 3: Escalate
2 consecutive drifts → escalate to a more capable model. The current model's "emotional state" is beyond its capacity to regulate — bring in a stronger reasoner.
2 consecutive drifts

These rules map directly to Anthropic's finding that monitoring emotion vectors enables early warning. The difference: CRT's monitoring operates at the behavioral level (output alignment with evidence) rather than the neural level (activation patterns). Both are valid measurement surfaces for the same underlying dynamics.

Production Implementation

Drift events are computed per-step by the alignment scorer, emitted as SSE events through the pipeline, and rendered in real-time in the frontend pipeline panel. All three governance rules are wired end-to-end: detection → event emission → response action.

Section 8

Emotional Routing

The practical output of emotion governance is emotion-aware model routing: selecting which model handles a request based on the system's current epistemic-emotional state. This is CRT's Layer 4 (epistemic routing) applied through the lens of functional emotion governance.

High Pressure
Route to Most Capable Model, Low Temperature
When cascade pressure is high or alignment is dropping, the system routes to its most capable model (cloud provider, larger context) with reduced temperature. This is the equivalent of "take a breath" — slow down, use the strongest available reasoning, reduce randomness.
Held Contradiction
Do Not Escalate — Retrieve Both Positions
When the relevant beliefs are in held contradiction, escalating to a more powerful model is counterproductive. Instead, the system retrieves both contradictory positions and presents the tension. Held contradictions are not errors to resolve — they are complexity to preserve.
Calm / Warm
Local Model Sufficient
When alignment is high, belief state is stable, and no contradictions are active, a local model is sufficient. This reduces cost and latency without sacrificing safety. The "calm" regime is the efficient regime.

This routing logic means the system's resource allocation is governed by its epistemic-emotional state. High-stakes moments get more compute. Stable moments get efficient processing. Contradictions get nuance instead of power. The result: both risk and cost are reduced simultaneously, because the system allocates resources where they are needed rather than uniformly.

Section 9

Implications & Future Work

For AI Safety

Governance architectures complement interpretability. Interpretability reveals the dynamics; governance controls them. Neither is sufficient alone. A system with interpretability but no governance can see the problem but not respond. A system with governance but no interpretability is flying blind with good reflexes. The complete safety picture requires both.

For Deployment

Emotion-aware routing reduces both risk and cost. By allocating expensive compute only when the system's epistemic state warrants it — high cascade pressure, active contradictions, sustained drift — operators can maintain safety guarantees while reducing average inference cost. The calm regime is the cheap regime.

For Regulation

The EU AI Act (Article 50) requires transparency about AI system capabilities and limitations. Functional emotion governance provides the infrastructure for this transparency: auditable belief/speech gaps, real-time drift monitoring, and documented governance rules that explain why the system made specific routing and response decisions.

Future Directions

  • Direct vector integration — When Anthropic exposes emotion vector readouts via API, CRT can incorporate neural-level measurements alongside behavioral measurements, creating a dual-layer monitoring surface.
  • User-side emotional mirror — A bidirectional loop where the user's emotional context (inferred from interaction patterns) informs the model's governance state, creating a full emotion-aware interaction loop.
  • Cross-model emotion transfer — Using CRT's regime taxonomy to predict how a task's emotional demands will interact with a specific model's response morphology, enabling preemptive routing before problems manifest.
  • Middleware extraction — Packaging the governance layer as an OpenAI-compatible proxy sidecar, enabling any agentic system to add emotion governance without modifying its model stack.
Section 10

Conclusion

Anthropic proved the neural dynamics exist. 171 functional emotion vectors, causally shaping behavior, confirmed via steering experiments. This is the interpretability layer: understanding what is happening inside the model.

CRT provides the governance architecture. Cascade damping, drift detection, belief/speech separation, structural posture gates, regime-aware routing. This is the control layer: determining what to do about what is happening.

The bridge between them is precise:

  • Interpretability tells you WHAT is happening (the model is in a "desperate" state)
  • Governance tells you WHAT TO DO about it (trigger cascade firewalls, escalate to a calmer model, halt and re-ground)

Neither layer is complete without the other. An interpretability system without governance is a dashboard with no controls. A governance system without interpretability is a control panel with no sensors. The position of this paper is that every agentic AI system needs both layers, and that the convergence between Anthropic's neural-level findings and CRT's behavioral-level architecture demonstrates these are not competing approaches but complementary components of a complete safety architecture.

Position
Interpretability + Governance = Complete Safety Picture
Understanding what the model feels is necessary. Knowing what to do about it is sufficient. Both together are the minimum viable safety architecture for agentic AI.
