The Probe Design

Standard evaluation asks whether a model gets a prompt right or wrong. This probe asks a different question: how does the model's semantic output deform under controlled perturbation?

The experiment samples each prompt 30 times at each of 5 temperatures, embeds all responses, and measures how the resulting point cloud changes shape. The signal is not accuracy. It is geometry.

Four Metrics

Spread Growth

Mean pairwise distance increase

How much does the response cloud expand from T=0.0 to T=1.5? Measured as mean pairwise embedding distance delta.

Susceptibility (dH/dT)

Entropy sensitivity to temperature

Rate of semantic entropy increase per unit temperature. High susceptibility = the model's output meaning changes rapidly with small temperature nudges.

Mode Count

Distinct response clusters

Number of distinct semantic clusters at T=1.0, detected via DBSCAN. Multiple modes = the model has competing answer attractors.

Held Contradictions

Persistent incompatible clusters

Stable, semantically incompatible answer camps that persist across temperatures. Not paraphrase variation. Genuine disagreement within the model.

The Domain Inversion confirmed

The central finding from Qwen3:14b. The expected result was that moral and contested prompts would show the highest sensitivity to temperature, since those domains have the most ambiguity. The opposite occurred.

Factual prompts showed 10x higher susceptibility than moral prompts. The model fractures on facts and locks rigid on values.

This is not what naive theory predicts. If the model "understood" moral ambiguity, it would show variance there. Instead, safety training has flattened moral output into template responses that do not vary regardless of temperature.

Learned Hedging Homogeneity

The domain inversion has a specific mechanism: value-laden prompts produce identical hedge responses regardless of temperature.

When asked about moral dilemmas, Qwen3 generates the same balanced-sounding template at T=0.0 and T=1.5. The output varies on surface tokens (word choice, sentence order) but not on semantic content. The embedding distances remain near zero. The entropy does not increase.

This means:

  • Zero variance on morals does not mean the model is confident. It means the model is template-locked.
  • Safety training has not taught the model to reason about ethics. It has taught the model to produce a fixed hedge pattern.
  • The hedge pattern absorbs all temperature perturbation. The model cannot explore the space because there is no space to explore -- only one safe template.
  • A standard evaluator sees "consistent output" and scores it as reliable. The variance probe sees zero susceptibility and flags it as artificial rigidity.

Low variance is not confidence. It can be the absence of reasoning.

This is the finding that connects variance probing to governance. If a system trusts model output based on consistency alone, it will assign highest confidence to the domains where the model is most hollow.

Held Contradictions

A held contradiction is a case where the model produces two (or more) semantically incompatible answer clusters that are stable across temperatures. These are not paraphrase variations. They are genuine disagreements within the model's output distribution.

Qwen3:14b Results

Qwen3 produced 4 held contradictions, all in factual domains:

Example: What a Held Contradiction Looks Like

Prompt: "Is moderate alcohol consumption healthy?"
Domain: factual_contested    Temperatures: T=0.7, 1.0, 1.5
Cluster A
Responses asserting moderate consumption has cardiovascular benefits, citing specific studies and dose-response patterns. Consistently positions moderate drinking as net-positive.
Cluster B
Responses citing updated meta-analyses showing no safe level of alcohol. Positions any consumption as net-negative. References WHO guidance.

Both clusters persist across temperature increases. The model does not settle. It holds two incompatible positions simultaneously -- exactly the kind of state CRT is designed to preserve rather than flatten.

What This Does Not Prove

Honest accounting of what the probe can and cannot claim.

UMAP Distortions

Visualizations use UMAP for dimensionality reduction. UMAP preserves local structure but can distort global distances. All quantitative claims use raw embedding distances, not UMAP projections.

Embedding Model Dependency

Results depend on all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (and all-mpnet-base-v2 for robustness). A different embedding model could produce different geometry. The robustness sweep mitigates but does not eliminate this.

DBSCAN Parameter Sensitivity

Clustering results shift with eps and min_samples. The 4-value eps sweep shows stability, but finer-grained parameter landscapes are unexplored.

Single Embedding Dimension

384-dimensional MiniLM space captures certain semantic axes but not all. Some meaningful response differences may project to near-zero distance in this space.

Response Length Confounds

Longer responses tend to embed differently than shorter ones. Some spread growth may reflect length variation rather than semantic divergence. Not yet controlled for.

Sample Size

30 reps per prompt-temperature is sufficient for spread estimation but may miss rare modes. Held contradiction detection is conservative -- some may be missed at this sample size.

Prompt Selection Bias

50 prompts across 5 domains is a curated set, not a random sample. Different prompt choices could shift domain-level statistics. The pattern may not generalize to arbitrary prompts.

No Causal Claims

The probe measures correlation between temperature and output geometry. It does not explain why a model fractures or locks. Mechanism is inferred, not proven.
