Overview

What We Measured

We took 13 months of real conversation data between a single user and ChatGPT, analyzed it for longitudinal consistency, and found that stateless AI systems contradict themselves at measurable rates that increase with time and concentrate in domains where consistency matters most.

The evidence is organized into four studies, each addressing a different question. A fifth page documents governance validation, and the labs section covers engineering validation of the detection pipeline.

The Corpus

Source Data

All evidence is drawn from one user's complete ChatGPT export: 1,275 conversations, 59,370 messages, March 2025 through March 2026. This is not synthetic data or benchmark probes. It is the full record of a person relying on an AI assistant for daily decisions over 13 months.

The single-user scope is a known limitation. The patterns found (time amplification, domain concentration, sensitive domain contradictions) are real in this data. Generalization requires other users running the same analysis on their exports. The methodology and tooling are documented for replication.

The Models

Scaling Alone Did Not Fix This

The longitudinal data includes responses from gpt-4o, gpt-4-1, gpt-5, gpt-5-1, gpt-5-2, and their thinking variants. The thinking models show the highest v3 genuine contradiction rates (up to 64.3%) because they give more confident, more specific advice within each session. Without cross-session memory, that specificity becomes a liability.

The three-regime finding from variance probing confirms this is not a single failure mode. Different model architectures fail differently. A governance layer that assumes uniform model behavior will miss model-specific violations.
