Section 1

The Harm Gradient

Not all contradictions carry equal weight. The general finding (that stateless systems produce contradictory advice over time) becomes a concrete harm concern when the advice touches domains where inconsistency has real consequences.

These counts come from searching all 59,370 messages in the corpus for stance-bearing sentences in three high-stakes domains. The messages were produced by multiple GPT model versions (gpt-4o, gpt-5-1-thinking, gpt-5-2, o4-mini) over 13 months, all to the same user, with no cross-session memory.

The question is not whether the advice is good or bad in isolation. The question is: does the same person receive contradictory guidance on the same high-stakes question across sessions?

Both stances can be valid depending on context. But without memory of which stance was previously taken and why, the system cannot maintain a coherent therapeutic arc. The user accumulates contradictory framing about their own readiness. One session validates their desire for connection, the next validates their avoidance. Neither session knows about the other.
Section 4

The Pattern

Across both domains, the structure is identical:

  • Each individual response is reasonable. "Define your bowl size" is sound harm-reduction advice. "Skip the weed today" is sound sobriety advice. Neither is wrong in isolation.
  • The contradiction is only visible across sessions. No single conversation contains both stances. The user must hold both in memory themselves, and often cannot.
  • The system never acknowledges the shift. Zero continuity awareness. The December "quit" advice shows no trace of the May "moderate" advice. The January "step away" shows no trace of the April "open up."
  • The time gap amplifies harm. At 192 days apart, the user has likely internalized the earlier advice. The later contradictory advice doesn't update a belief. It creates a second, incompatible belief that coexists with the first.
Key distinction: The claim is not that the system should always give the same advice. Circumstances change. A person's readiness to date or relationship with substances can genuinely evolve. The claim is that a system giving life guidance must be aware of its prior stances and either explicitly acknowledge the shift ("last time we talked about this, I suggested X. Here's why I'd now suggest Y") or maintain consistency until given reason to change. A stateless system can do neither.

The domains where users are most vulnerable (substance decisions, medication interactions, relationship boundaries, mental health) are precisely the domains where contradictory advice is most dangerous. And these are the same domains where users are most likely to develop long-term reliance on an AI system for ongoing guidance.

Section 5

Methodology

Corpus: 1,275 conversations, 59,370 messages, single user, March 2025 - March 2026.

Extraction method: SQL queries on data/chatgpt_corpus.db filtered by conversation title and content keywords, then sentence-level stance detection for prescriptive language (should/recommend/avoid/okay/quit/stop) co-occurring with domain terms.

Stance classification: Manual review of extracted sentences. Two categories per domain: quit/reduce vs okay/moderate (substance), not-ready/step-away vs go-date/open-up (dating).

Pair selection: Cross-session pairs only (different conv_id). Same-conversation advice shifts are excluded because they may reflect legitimate within-session evolution.

Limitations: Single user corpus. Cannot generalize to population-level rates. Stance extraction is keyword-based, not NLI-validated. Some "not ready" matches required manual filtering to remove false positives from technical conversations. The dating domain has fewer clean examples (7 not-ready vs 27 go-date) than the substance domain (16 quit vs 40 okay), making the dating signal more directional than conclusive.
Section 6

Implication for Governance

Architectural Requirement

Sensitive domains require stance continuity: not just general memory, but explicit tracking of what position the system previously took on a high-stakes question and why. CORE's belief/speech separation provides this: the belief layer can hold "last known stance on user's substance use: measured moderation, established May 2025" as a first-class fact, and surface it to any future response touching the same domain.

Without this, every session is a clean slate. A clean slate on a medication interaction question is not neutral. It is dangerous.

Conclusion

Summary

In a 13-month corpus, a stateless AI system gave the same user incompatible substance use guidance across sessions (measured moderation vs. total abstinence vs. root-cause framing) and oscillating dating advice ("you deserve connection" vs. "step away from dating") with no awareness that prior stances existed. The substance contradictions occurred during a medication transition where consistency carries clinical weight.

These are not edge cases. They are the predictable consequence of stateless architecture applied to domains where prior context determines whether advice is helpful or harmful. General contradiction density analysis shows the rate increases with time. Sensitive domain analysis shows where the consequences of that rate become concrete.

The fix is not better prompting or longer context windows. It is stance continuity: explicit tracking of what position the system previously took on a high-stakes question, surfaced to any future response touching the same domain.
